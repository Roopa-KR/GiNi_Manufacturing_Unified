GiNi Manufacturing
Supervisor App and Operator App
Presentation-Style Talk Guide

Purpose
-------
This guide is written for someone presenting GiNi for the first time. It explains
what the application is, who uses each app, how the apps share information, and
how to demonstrate the main manufacturing workflow clearly.

Use this as:

  1. A learning guide before the presentation.
  2. A speaker script during the presentation.
  3. A checklist for preparing a live demonstration.
  4. A source of short answers for likely questions.

Important product disclaimer
----------------------------
GiNi is a synthetic manufacturing demonstration for transformer-tank production.
The people, plant, tanks, work orders, and history shown in the demo are fictional.
It is not connected to a real factory, is not Siemens-approved, and is not
production software.

The two user-facing applications are:

  - Supervisor App: http://localhost:5174
  - Operator App:   http://localhost:5173

The applications communicate through:

  - Express API:    http://localhost:5000
  - MongoDB:        shared operational database
  - Python engine:  background calculations, forecasts, alerts, points, recovery,
                    and verification


1. The one-minute explanation
-----------------------------
Suggested opening:

"GiNi connects shop-floor execution with supervisory decision-making. The
Operator App is where a worker or inspector performs real work: they clock in,
accept a task, confirm the correct tank, record milestones, pause or request
help, finish the operation, and complete an inspection when assigned. The
Supervisor App is where a supervisor sees the whole plant, configures and
dispatches work, assigns people, follows quality and materials, responds to
alerts, evaluates recovery options, and verifies whether predictions matched
actual results. Both apps use the same validated API and the same manufacturing
records, so an operator action immediately becomes visible to supervisors and a
supervisor decision changes what the operator can do next."

The central idea is traceability:

  work order -> batch -> tank identity -> route operation -> worker activity
  -> inspection -> evidence -> release, rework, or scrap -> final passport


2. What problem does GiNi solve?
---------------------------------
Manufacturing teams need more than a list of tasks. They need to know:

  - What should be produced?
  - Which tanks are in progress and where are they?
  - Who is working on each operation?
  - Is the work proceeding according to the route and standard time?
  - Has the operation passed its required quality gate?
  - What evidence supports the quality decision?
  - Which materials, equipment, skills, and stations are constraining work?
  - Which orders are at risk of missing their cutoff?
  - What action is feasible without damaging another order?
  - Can every decision be explained after the event?

GiNi answers these questions with two different experiences:

  - The operator experience is focused, task-oriented, and suitable for a
    workstation, tablet, or phone.
  - The supervisor experience is plant-wide, analytical, and decision-oriented.


3. The mental model: objects and states
----------------------------------------
Before showing screens, explain these terms.

Factory
  The configured plant, timezone, work centres, stations, shifts, skills,
  equipment, materials, products, routes, and inspection checklists.

Work order
  The demand to produce a required number of GOOD tanks for a customer or
  reference by a due date and time.

Batch
  A planned or released grouping of tanks within a work order. A batch can be
  split while preserving membership history.

Tank / unit
  One physical manufactured tank with its own permanent identity and serial.
  A tank is not a reusable vessel in the software; it is the product being
  manufactured.

Route
  The ordered operations required to make the product. A route includes
  operation names, work centres, required skills, crew limits, labour and
  elapsed-time standards, milestones, materials, and inspection gates.

Task
  The executable assignment for a worker at a particular operation and tank.

Work centre and station
  A work centre is an area or capability. A station is a specific place or
  resource inside that work centre.

Inspection gate
  A quality checkpoint on the route. Finishing production work does not pass
  the gate; an authorized inspector must submit the inspection.

Tank passport
  The traceability record for one tank. It brings together identity, route
  progress, people and labour, inspections, readings, photographs, holds,
  rework, material traceability, scans, points, and the next expected action.

Good output
  An accepted tank. Required quantity means accepted GOOD tanks, not attempts.
  Rework and scrap never count as good output.

Session
  A simulation branch or, in fresh-factory mode, the permanent real-time factory
  session. Sessions isolate their operational records from one another.


4. How the apps and services interact
--------------------------------------
Use this simple architecture explanation:

  Supervisor App ----\
                       -> Express API -> MongoDB
  Operator App -------/
                              ^
                              |
                       Python engine

The browser does not directly change manufacturing state. It sends a validated
command to the API. The API checks authentication, permissions, ownership,
current state, route rules, and idempotency. It then records the event and state
transition transactionally. The Python engine reads committed records and
publishes derived information such as:

  - Labour metrics
  - Route and order forecasts
  - Cutoff bands
  - Variance evaluations
  - Alerts and notification outbox entries
  - Points ledger calculations
  - Recovery options
  - Verification results

The browser displays the latest published projection. If the engine is
recalculating or unavailable, the application shows freshness/status information
instead of pretending that the value is zero.

Important distinction:

  Observed = what has actually been recorded.
  Forecast = what the engine predicts from current data and standards.

Always point out this distinction in the Supervisor App.


5. Who uses which app?
-----------------------
Supervisor App users

  - Administrator: factory setup, workers, sessions, rules, audit, and system
    administration.
  - Area supervisor: dispatch, assignments, workforce visibility, and
    operational supervision.
  - Shift manager: operational supervision, transfers, and recovery approvals.
  - Quality supervisor: quality decisions, dispositions, investigations, and
    evidence review.

Operator App users

  - Production operator: assigned production work, milestones, pauses, help,
    handovers, incoming defects, and personal progress.
  - Quality inspector: assigned independent inspections, readings, answers,
    photographs, and submission.
  - Maintenance technician: maintenance-related tasks and equipment actions
    allowed by the role.
  - Material handler: material movements and deliveries allowed by the role.

Some supervisory roles can sign in to both apps, but the purpose is different:
the Supervisor App is for plant control; the Operator App is for executing a
worker's own assigned activity.

The API is the authority for permissions. Hiding a button in the browser is not
the security boundary.


6. Supervisor App: what it is
-----------------------------
Suggested explanation:

"The Supervisor App is the control tower. It is not just a dashboard. It is
where the supervisor understands the current plant state, decides what should
happen next, and records controlled administrative or quality decisions."

Supervisor navigation groups:

  Observe
    Overview
    Orders & Batches
    Work Centers
    Workforce

  Quality & decisions
    Quality & Rework
    Alerts
    Recovery
    Verify

  Plant
    Materials & Equipment
    Administration

  Fresh factory setup
    Factory setup
    Dispatch & assign


7. Supervisor App screen-by-screen
-----------------------------------
7.1 Login and header

The supervisor signs in using the administrator account or a worker account with
supervisory permissions. The header shows:

  - GiNi Supervisor branding
  - Demo or fresh-factory context
  - Session mode
  - Data freshness
  - Tank search
  - Simulation clock controls where permitted
  - Current plant/simulation time
  - Signed-in user

In synthetic interactive mode, an administrator may:

  - Play or pause the shared simulation clock.
  - Change speed, for example x1 through x120.
  - Recalculate projections.

In Manual Team Test mode, the clock is real time. There is no Play/Pause/Speed
control. This is a useful point to make because the two operating modes behave
differently.

7.2 Overview

Purpose:
  Give a measured plant-wide snapshot.

Show:

  - Accepted good tanks
  - Active work in progress
  - Scrapped tanks
  - Quality holds
  - People present and productively working
  - Work-centre summaries
  - Queue and capacity information
  - Equipment constraints
  - Forecast lines and limiting constraints

Talk track:

"The overview separates what has happened from what may happen. The counts are
observed operational facts. The forecast explains where the current plan is
likely to finish and what is limiting it. A forecast is not a completed result."

7.3 Orders & Batches

Purpose:
  Manage demand and see progress by customer/order, line, batch, and tank.

Typical actions:

  - Create a work order.
  - Select a released product.
  - Enter required GOOD quantity.
  - Set due date and time.
  - Define production batches.
  - Release a batch and create permanent tank identities.
  - Open a line and inspect its tanks.
  - Split a batch while preserving membership history.
  - Print tank labels.

Key explanation:

"The order quantity is the number of accepted good tanks. If one tank is
scrapped, the order still needs the same good quantity. A replacement gets a
new tank identity and is linked to the original."

7.4 Tank Passport

Open a tank by selecting its ID or serial from Orders, Work Centers, Quality,
Alerts, or the search box.

Use the passport to explain the complete history:

  - Identity and serial
  - Current state and location
  - Route progress and operation attempts
  - Worker participation and labour
  - Inspection status and readings
  - Photo/evidence records
  - Holds and dispositions
  - Rework and reinspection
  - Material and equipment traceability
  - Label scans
  - Points and next expected action

This is the best screen to demonstrate that the application is traceable rather
than just displaying isolated status cards.

7.5 Work Centers

Purpose:
  View the plant by area and station.

Discuss:

  - Tanks currently at or queued for each centre.
  - Station assignments and capacity.
  - People working, waiting, or on break.
  - Equipment availability.
  - Work-centre bottlenecks.
  - Tank links leading to passports.

7.6 Workforce

Purpose:
  See who is available and what they are doing.

Useful filters:

  - Working
  - On break
  - Off shift

Open an individual worker to see:

  - Role and qualifications
  - Home work centre and shift
  - Current task
  - Attendance/timeline
  - Labour activity
  - Points information where permitted

Important wording:

"Last known" telemetry is not the same as "idle." Missing telemetry must not be
interpreted as proof that a person is inactive.

7.7 Quality & Rework

Purpose:
  Manage inspection outcomes and quality decisions.

Show:

  - Tanks awaiting inspection
  - Failed inspections
  - Quality holds
  - Rework candidates
  - Reinspection state
  - Scrap decisions
  - Replacement requests
  - Investigation records

Quality rules to emphasize:

  - An incomplete inspection is not a failure and not a release.
  - A failed inspection places the tank on hold.
  - Rework requires an authorized disposition.
  - Rework normally returns to the same station where appropriate.
  - An alternate station needs approval and a reason.
  - Repeated failure escalates to a quality supervisor or administrator.
  - There is never an automatic forced pass.

7.8 Alerts

Purpose:
  Show evolving operational conditions and their lifecycle.

An alert can move through:

  Healthy -> FIRING -> ACKNOWLEDGED while still firing
            -> RECOVERING/RESOLVED after the cause is corrected

Acknowledging an alert does not resolve its cause.

The system evaluates causes such as:

  - Stalled assignment
  - Regional workload imbalance
  - Material velocity or shortage
  - Location dwell
  - Congestion
  - Replenishment delay
  - Manufacturing variance rules

Explain that scenario controls inject a physical cause, not a flag directly. The
engine observes the cause and evaluates the rule. This makes the demo more
realistic and preserves cause/effect.

7.9 Recovery

Purpose:
  Help a supervisor compare feasible actions for an at-risk order line.

Possible options include:

  - Transfer a worker
  - Call in a relief worker
  - Approve overtime
  - Release a replacement
  - Resequence work
  - Expedite
  - Take no action as the baseline

The engine checks constraints such as:

  - Recipient qualification at arrival
  - Donor minimum crew
  - Queue coverage
  - Material and equipment availability
  - Impact on other orders
  - Whether the option damages the source operation

Suggested explanation:

"Recovery is not a list of guesses. The application compares options, explains
infeasibility, and rejects an action that would solve one line by damaging
another."

7.10 Verify

Purpose:
  Compare a stored proposal or forecast with observed results.

Typical flow:

  1. Propose an action.
  2. Approve it with the required authority.
  3. Start the action.
  4. Let the plant continue.
  5. Verify predicted versus observed result.

If the underlying situation changes before approval, the application can reject
the stale approval and require a new simulation.

7.11 Materials & Equipment

Purpose:
  Track production constraints.

Materials:

  - Material definitions
  - Lots and quantities
  - Receipts
  - Requests
  - Deliveries
  - Shortages and need-by times

Equipment:

  - Equipment identity and station
  - Availability
  - Breakdown
  - Repair

Explain that stock is not unlimited. A released order may exist while starting
work is blocked by missing stock, station, equipment, or qualified people.

7.12 Administration

Main administrative areas:

  - Workers and accounts
  - Sessions and simulation
  - Factory setup
  - Rule settings
  - Jobs and engine heartbeat
  - Notifications
  - Import/reconciliation
  - Audit

Worker creation:

  - Creates a permanent worker ID and login.
  - Assigns role, shift, home centre, and qualifications.
  - Stores the PIN as a hash.
  - Does not automatically clock the worker in.
  - Does not create fake tasks, history, or points.

Audit:
  Every important administrative change is recorded with who, when, and
  before/after context. This is essential when explaining accountability.

7.13 Factory setup and Dispatch & assign

In a fresh factory, setup proceeds in this order:

  1. Factory name, site, timezone, serial prefix, operating mode.
  2. Work centres and stations.
  3. Skills.
  4. Shifts and working days.
  5. Equipment and materials.
  6. Products and routes.
  7. Inspection checklists.
  8. Workers and qualifications.
  9. Work order.
  10. Batch release.
  11. Dispatch and crew assignment.

The setup checklist identifies Not configured, Partial, and Ready areas.

Dispatch shows readiness and blockers before assignment. The supervisor can
assign a tank's next operation, station, and crew, then assign an independent
qualified inspector after production work reaches the inspection gate.


8. Operator App: what it is
---------------------------
Suggested explanation:

"The Operator App is intentionally narrower than the Supervisor App. It puts
the next correct action in front of the person doing the work, while preserving
the records needed for production and quality traceability."

Operator navigation:

  - Work
  - Queue
  - Progress
  - Profile

Additional task routes:

  - Inspection
  - Incoming defect report
  - Task details
  - Tank passport


9. Operator App screen-by-screen
---------------------------------
9.1 Login

The worker signs in using employee/login ID and PIN. PINs are text values, so
leading zeroes matter. For example, 004211 is not the same as 4211.

Signing in does not:

  - Clock the worker in.
  - Start a task.
  - Restart paused work.
  - Create history.

This is a useful safety and audit point to demonstrate.

9.2 Header and scan

The header shows:

  - GiNi Operator branding
  - Session or factory mode
  - Simulation/plant time
  - Tank/station scan action
  - Signed-in worker

Scanning can use:

  - Camera where supported
  - Handheld barcode scanner
  - Typed serial or ID

Every tank or station scan is saved in the tank passport. If configured, the
correct tank scan is required before work or inspection can start.

9.3 Work

The Work screen is the worker's primary home screen.

It can show:

  - Current assigned task
  - Tank and serial
  - Work order, line, and batch
  - Operation and work centre
  - Crew
  - Instruction revision
  - Milestones
  - Standard and eligible time
  - Current task state
  - Points as known

Typical action sequence:

  1. Clock in.
  2. Open the assigned task.
  3. Confirm the tank by scan or entry.
  4. Start work.
  5. Complete milestones.
  6. Pause or resume with a reason when needed.
  7. Request help if blocked.
  8. Handover if work must move to another person.
  9. Finish work.

Finishing production work changes the tank to awaiting inspection where a gate
exists. It does not pass the inspection and does not release the tank.

9.4 Queue

Purpose:
  Show eligible work that the worker can accept or inspect.

The queue respects:

  - Qualification
  - Role
  - Assignment
  - Station and route rules
  - Current tank state
  - Inspection independence

It is not a free-for-all task list. The API validates every action.

9.5 Progress

Purpose:
  Show the worker's own recorded progress.

May include:

  - Completed tasks
  - Labour categories
  - Crew shares
  - Time points
  - Pending points
  - Ledger lines
  - Reversals or corrections

Points are ledger-based. The total should reconcile to the individual rows.
Crew time is split; it is not duplicated for every crew member.

9.6 Profile

Purpose:
  Show the worker's identity, role, attendance, qualifications, and personal
  status/actions available to that worker.

The app may also use a human-control lease in an interactive demonstration so
that a signed-in tester controls the worker rather than a simulated agent.

9.7 Inspection

An inspector sees an assigned inspection only when the tank is ready and the
assignment is valid.

Inspection flow:

  1. Open the assigned inspection.
  2. Confirm the correct tank if scanning is required.
  3. Start the gate inspection.
  4. Answer checklist questions.
  5. Enter readings and units where required.
  6. Add required photographs.
  7. Wait for photo evidence to reach Verified.
  8. Submit the inspection.

Possible result:

  - INCOMPLETE: required information is missing; no release.
  - PASS: tank can proceed to release/next route step.
  - FAIL: tank is held for quality disposition.

An inspection uses the checklist version that was current when it started. This
protects the historical meaning of an in-progress inspection if the checklist
is later revised.

9.8 Incoming defect report

An operator or inspector can report a defect found on a received tank. This:

  - Places the tank on hold.
  - Opens an investigation against the earlier operation.
  - Does not immediately assign blame.
  - Does not immediately change points.

Later, an authorized quality user can confirm a cause such as worker,
equipment, or another category. Worker responsibility requires valid evidence
and shares; equipment cause does not deduct points from a worker. Corrections
create linked reversals rather than silently rewriting history.

9.9 Tank passport

The operator can open the passport for the current tank. The operator view is
appropriately limited: it does not expose other people's private minutes,
attribution, or supervisory-only information.


10. The end-to-end workflow to present
---------------------------------------
Use one tank as the story. Keep repeating its identity so the audience follows
the lifecycle.

Step 1: Configure the factory

  - Define work centres and stations.
  - Define skills and shifts.
  - Add equipment and materials.
  - Create a product route.
  - Create inspection checklist versions.
  - Release the product.

Message:
  "The application refuses an incomplete product release and lists what is
  missing. Configuration is not silently assumed."

Step 2: Create people

  - Create a production operator.
  - Create a qualified quality inspector.
  - Assign roles, shifts, home centres, and qualifications.

Message:
  "A person is not automatically clocked in and does not receive fabricated
  work just because an account was created."

Step 3: Create and release demand

  - Create a work order for a required number of GOOD tanks.
  - Add due date/time and batch sizes.
  - Release the batch.
  - Show the permanent tank ID and serial.
  - Print the tank label if available.

Step 4: Dispatch

  - Select the tank's next operation.
  - Confirm materials, station, equipment, and qualified crew.
  - Assign the production crew.

Message:
  "The supervisor sees blockers before the operator is asked to start."

Step 5: Execute production in the Operator App

  - Operator logs in.
  - Operator clocks in.
  - Operator confirms the tank.
  - Operator starts the task.
  - Operator completes milestones.
  - Operator pauses/resumes or requests help if needed.
  - Operator finishes work.

Message:
  "Finish means the production operation is complete. It is deliberately not
  the same as quality approval."

Step 6: Assign and perform inspection

  - Supervisor assigns an independent qualified inspector.
  - Inspector opens the assigned inspection in the Operator App.
  - Inspector completes checklist, readings, and evidence.
  - Inspector submits PASS or FAIL.

Step 7A: Passing path

  - PASS is stored with readings and evidence.
  - The tank becomes eligible for release/next route step.
  - Supervisor sees accepted progress and the passport.

Step 7B: Failing path

  - FAIL places the tank on quality hold.
  - Supervisor opens Quality & Rework.
  - Authorized user decides repairable, scrap, or further review.
  - Repairable work creates a new attempt on the same tank.
  - The tank is reinspected.
  - A repeated failure escalates.

Step 8: Scrap and replacement

  - Scrap is permanent and never counts as good output.
  - Request and approve a replacement.
  - Replacement receives a new identity linked to the original.
  - The order's required good quantity remains unchanged.

Step 9: Monitor risk and recover

  - Open Overview, Alerts, Orders, and Recovery.
  - Explain observed counts versus forecast.
  - Simulate or observe an at-risk line.
  - Compare feasible transfer, overtime, replacement, resequence, expedite, or
    no-action options.
  - Approve and start a selected action if authorized.

Step 10: Verify

  - Compare the stored before/after projection with recorded results.
  - Explain that stale proposals must be recalculated rather than blindly
    applied.

---------------------------------------
Suggested duration: 25 to 35 minutes.

Part A - Context and architecture (3 minutes)

Say:
  "GiNi has one manufacturing model and two focused user experiences. Both
  apps use the same API and database. The engine calculates derived information
  and the API enforces every state transition."

Show:
  - The two URLs.
  - The architecture diagram verbally.
  - The synthetic-data banner.

Part B - Supervisor plant view (5 minutes)

Show:
  1. Overview.
  2. Orders & Batches.
  3. Work Centers.
  4. Workforce.
  5. A tank passport.

Say:
  "I can move from a plant-wide count to one tank's complete trace without
  losing identity."

Part C - Operator execution (5 minutes)

Show:
  1. Operator login.
  2. Work card.
  3. Confirm tank scan.
  4. Start work.
  5. Milestone completion.
  6. Pause/resume or help.
  7. Finish work.

Say:
  "The operator is guided through the next valid action and does not need the
  full complexity of the supervisor view."

Part D - Quality handoff (5 minutes)

Show:
  1. Supervisor assigns inspector.
  2. Inspector opens inspection.
  3. Checklist and readings.
  4. Photo upload and verification.
  5. PASS or FAIL.
  6. Quality hold or release state.

Say:
  "Production completion and quality release are separate controlled events."

Part E - Rework and traceability (3 minutes)

Show:
  1. Quality hold.
  2. Disposition.
  3. Rework attempt or scrap.
  4. Passport history.

Part F - Alerts, recovery, and verification (5 minutes)

Show:
  1. Alert lifecycle.
  2. Recovery options and feasibility reasons.
  3. Verify page.

Close with:
  "The outcome is not only a completed tank. It is a defensible record of what
  happened, who acted, what evidence was captured, and why the next decision was
  allowed."

13. Important rules to say accurately
--------------------------------------
Use these exact ideas when answering questions:

  - Login does not clock a worker in.
  - Creating a worker does not create history or tasks.
  - Finishing work is not passing inspection.
  - Passing inspection is not the same as starting the next operation.
  - An incomplete inspection is a hold, not a failure and not a release.
  - A failed inspection goes on quality hold.
  - Rework uses a new attempt on the same tank.
  - Scrap never counts as good output.
  - A replacement receives a new tank identity.
  - A tank passport preserves the complete sequence of events.
  - Required photos must be uploaded and verified before submission.
  - Inspector assignment requires qualification and independence.
  - Acknowledging an alert does not resolve its cause.
  - A forecast is not observed output.
  - Unknown forecast is not the same as on-time.
  - The system does not invent history to force an alert.
  - Every command is validated by the API.
  - Administrative and quality actions are permission-controlled and audited.
  - Sessions are isolated; records from another branch do not leak into the
    current session.


14. Likely audience questions and answers
------------------------------------------
Q: What is the main difference between the two apps?

A:
  The Operator App helps one person execute an assigned task or inspection. The
  Supervisor App helps authorized users configure, dispatch, monitor, decide,
  recover, and audit across the plant.

Q: Do supervisors and operators see the same information?

A:
  They share the same underlying manufacturing state, but the screens are
  role-specific. Operators see their own work and appropriate tank information.
  Supervisors see plant-wide operational, quality, workforce, and decision data.

Q: Does finishing a task automatically release the tank?

A:
  No. Finishing production moves the tank to the required inspection state.
  An independent qualified inspector must complete the gate, and the release
  rules then determine whether the route can continue.

Q: What happens when inspection fails?

A:
  The tank is held. An authorized quality user decides whether it is repairable,
  should be scrapped, or needs further review. Repair creates a new attempt on
  the same tank and requires reinspection.

Q: How are photographs handled?

A:
  Evidence is uploaded, then verified. A required photograph must reach the
  verified state before the inspection can be submitted.

Q: Can any worker take any task from the queue?

A:
  No. Qualification, role, assignment, current state, station, and other route
  rules are checked by the API.

Q: Are alerts manually entered?

A:
  A scenario console can inject a physical cause for a demonstration, such as
  equipment interruption or material shortage. The engine then evaluates the
  cause and creates the alert lifecycle. The user does not directly switch a
  flag on.

Q: What does ACK mean?

A:
  ACK means someone has acknowledged the alert. It does not mean the cause has
  been corrected. The alert can remain FIRING until the underlying condition
  clears.

Q: How trustworthy is the forecast?

A:
  It is a calculation based on recorded state, route standards, workforce,
  shifts, constraints, and engine rules. The UI labels it as a forecast and
  keeps the observed result separate. If there is insufficient information,
  the result can be UNKNOWN rather than falsely optimistic.

Q: What happens if the engine is temporarily unavailable?

A:
  The last published projection remains visible with freshness/status
  information. The app does not silently replace the result with zero. Once the
  engine is back, it recalculates from committed records.

Q: Why does the app require MongoDB as a replica set?

A:
  The API uses multi-document transactions for consistent command/state/event
  updates. The replica-set configuration supports those transactions.

Q: Can a work order be completed with fewer tanks if one is scrapped?

A:
  No. Required quantity means accepted good tanks. A scrapped tank does not
  satisfy demand; a replacement can be requested and receives a new identity.

Q: Can a supervisor change a past event?

A:
  The design preserves history. Corrections and reversals are recorded as linked
  records, and administrative changes are audited rather than silently erased.
